# HU-13 — Asynchronous Job Tracking, Retries, and Recovery

## User story
As an authorized ticket participant, I want to view the progress and outcome of asynchronous jobs related to a ticket, so I can understand assignment and email-notification processing, including failures.

## Context
HU-11 implements RabbitMQ-based automatic ticket assignment using a separate worker and priority queue. HU-12 implements asynchronous Gmail SMTP notifications for TICKET_CREATED, TICKET_ASSIGNED, TICKET_RESOLVED, and TICKET_CLOSED. Extend those implementations without changing approved ticket workflows, access rules, assignment selection, or notification recipients.

## Scope
1. Persist a distinct job record for every assignment request and notification event, associated with its ticket. Suggested Prisma model `AsyncJob` with UUID `id`, `ticketId`, `type` (`TICKET_ASSIGNMENT` or `EMAIL_NOTIFICATION`), nullable `event` (notification type), `status` (`PENDING`, `PROCESSING`, `COMPLETED`, `FAILED`), nullable `result` (`SUCCESS` or `SKIPPED`), `attempts`, `maxAttempts` (3), sanitized nullable `lastError`, timestamps, and any minimal internal scheduling/claim fields required for recovery. Adapt naming to existing conventions.
2. Persist a job before dispatch, and pass its unique ID in RabbitMQ messages. Each distinct notification event gets a separate job. Duplicate delivery of the same job ID must not create duplicate records or repeat already-completed business effects. Do not conflate ticket status with job status.
3. Update both workers to transition job state consistently. `PENDING` means awaiting delivery or retry; `PROCESSING` means claimed; `COMPLETED` means processed; `FAILED` means no further attempts. A valid no-op (already assigned, deleted/missing ticket, or no active technicians) is `COMPLETED` with `SKIPPED` and a safe reason, not a technical failure. For email, `SUCCESS` means SMTP accepted the message, not that the recipient read or received it.
4. Retry transient failures for **three total processing attempts**, initially, after 5 seconds, then after 15 seconds. Use broker-backed delayed retry (e.g., TTL/dead-letter retry queues) rather than sleeping or in-process timers. After the third unsuccessful attempt, mark the job `FAILED` and retain a sanitized error. Permanent failures should not be retried pointlessly; define and test the classification. Preserve existing priority behavior for new assignment messages.
5. Handle broker downtime when producing jobs: persist a pending dispatch in PostgreSQL and provide a small, documented mechanism to publish unpublished jobs after RabbitMQ recovers. Consider the race between publishing and recording publication; delivery may be at least once, so consumers must be idempotent. Do not falsely claim exactly-once delivery.
6. Recover work interrupted by worker termination: broker redelivery and/or bounded stale-claim recovery must allow a `PROCESSING` record to continue safely. Avoid permanently stranded `PROCESSING` jobs. Do not purge production queues or silently drop pending work.
7. Provide authenticated `GET /api/tickets/:id/jobs` returning a **plain JSON array**, newest first, containing safe fields such as `id`, `type`, `event`, `status`, `result`, `attempts`, `maxAttempts`, `lastError`, `createdAt`, `updatedAt`. Match HU-08 ticket visibility: admin any visible ticket; client own; technician assigned. Invalid UUID -> 400; missing, soft-deleted, or inaccessible ticket -> 404. Never expose SMTP credentials, full email contents, broker connection details, stack traces, or other sensitive internals.
8. Keep changes focused on backend/worker infrastructure and documentation. No Angular screens, global job listing, manual retry endpoint, new infrastructure service, or email-provider replacement. Frontend display is HU-16.

## Reliability boundaries and implementation guidance
- Inspect current HU-11/HU-12 code and `AGENTS.md` before editing. Preserve existing queue semantics and avoid broad rewrites.
- A job must correspond to a committed business event; avoid sending notification or assignment jobs for rolled-back transactions. Choose a pragmatic transactional outbox or equivalent durable dispatch mechanism appropriate for this small project, and document its limits.
- At-least-once delivery is expected. Prevent repeated ticket assignment and unnecessary repeat notifications wherever feasible. SMTP delivery cannot be guaranteed exactly once if a process crashes after SMTP accepts a message but before completion is persisted; explicitly document this limitation.
- Ensure retry scheduling, job creation, and state transitions are safe under duplicate messages and worker restarts. Use deterministic IDs or a unique constraint where appropriate to prevent duplicated job creation for the same event.
- Maintain readable logs with job ID, ticket ID, job type/event, status, attempt, and safe failure/skip reason. Do not log secrets or full email payloads.
- Keep retry and recovery processes lightweight and documented; prefer existing dependencies and infrastructure.

## Acceptance criteria
- Creating a ticket produces trackable assignment and creation-notification jobs; successful assignment produces its own notification job; resolving and closing produce separate notification jobs.
- `GET /api/tickets/:id/jobs` reports the correct set of jobs, statuses, attempt counts, and outcomes, respecting access rules and newest-first ordering.
- Successful work becomes `COMPLETED` / `SUCCESS`; valid no-ops become `COMPLETED` / `SKIPPED` with a meaningful safe reason.
- A simulated transient failure is retried no more than three total times, with approximately 5-second and 15-second broker-backed delays, then ends in `FAILED` if unresolved.
- Jobs created during RabbitMQ downtime remain durably pending and can be published after recovery without losing the original ticket operation.
- Worker termination/restart does not permanently strand jobs in `PROCESSING`; duplicate delivery does not repeat completed assignment effects.
- Existing HU-11 priority selection and HU-12 four email events continue to function.
- Automated tests mock SMTP and external failures; they do not send real emails or require real secrets. Add focused tests for permissions, state transitions, retries, duplicates, broker outage/recovery, and worker restart behavior.
